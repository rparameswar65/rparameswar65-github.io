# Machine Learning in Hiring – Game Reflection

## (a) Motivating factor(s) for adding a Machine Learning model

In the game, the main motivation for introducing a Machine Learning model was the pressure to scale hiring quickly. As the company grew, there were too many applications for one person to review carefully, and the CEO was pushed to “keep up” with demand. The ML system was presented as a way to speed up resume screening, reduce the hiring manager’s workload, and copy the “successful” decisions they had already made.

Leadership also liked the idea that an algorithm would feel more consistent and data-driven than manual screening, even though that assumption turned out to be risky.

---

## (b) “Good” situations in the game

There were some clear positive situations. After the ML model was introduced, the company could process many more resumes in less time, which helped them fill roles faster and grow the business. The decision process also appeared more standardized at first, since the same criteria were applied to every candidate instead of depending on the hiring manager’s mood or time constraints.

From a business perspective, this led to short-term gains such as improved hiring throughput and a perception that the company was becoming more efficient and technologically advanced by using AI tools.

---

## (c) “Bad” situations in the game

The game primarily highlights the negative consequences of relying on the model. Because the algorithm was trained on historical decisions and external data, it amplified existing human biases rather than removing them. Over time, it favored candidates from one city or background and systematically filtered out qualified individuals from another, resulting in a less diverse and unfair workforce.

This created ethical, legal, and reputational risks for the company, which could be accused of discrimination while deflecting responsibility onto “the algorithm.” Another major issue was the black-box nature of the system, making it difficult to understand or contest why certain candidates were preferred over others.

---

## (d) Advice to a business leader on when/how to implement ML in hiring

Based on the game, ML should be used cautiously and with strong safeguards. It can be helpful for supporting parts of the hiring process—such as ranking resumes or flagging promising candidates—especially when application volume is high, but it should not function as the sole decision-maker.

Before deployment, organizations should audit training data for bias, define fairness metrics, and continuously monitor outcomes across demographic groups. A human-in-the-loop approach is essential to review edge cases and provide candidates with an appeal mechanism. In addition, a cross-functional team involving HR, legal, data science, and ethics should oversee the system, and transparency with both leadership and applicants should be prioritized.

If an organization is not prepared to invest in this level of oversight and accountability, it is safer not to automate high-stakes hiring decisions using ML.
